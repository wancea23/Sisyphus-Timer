The idea

Student life works like the punishment of Sisyphus. You finish one exam and the next one is already waiting. Camus asks what to do once you notice that, and his answer is to keep pushing with your eyes open. This timer lets you try that answer instead of only reading about it.

Who was Sisyphus?

  1. I

    The king who chained Death

    Sisyphus was king of Ephyra, the city later called Corinth, and the craftiest man alive. When Death came for him, he put Death in chains, and for a while nobody on earth could die. The god of war had to come and set Death free.

    Homer tells us also that Sisyphus had put Death in chains.
  2. II

    The trick

    Before dying, he told his wife to leave his body unburied. In the underworld he complained about her neglect and was allowed back up to punish her. Once he saw the world again, he refused to return.

    But when he had seen again the face of this world, enjoyed water and sun, warm stones and the sea, he no longer wanted to go back to the infernal darkness.
  3. III

    The punishment

    Hermes dragged him back down. The gods gave him a rock and a hill: work that never ends and never matters. Camus sees in him the absurd hero.

    Sisyphus is the absurd hero. He is, as much through his passions as through his torture.

The absurd, in four steps

  1. I

    The absurd

    We want the world to make sense, and the world doesn’t answer. Camus calls that collision the absurd.

    The absurd is born of this confrontation between the human need and the unreasonable silence of the world.
  2. II

    The why arrives

    Lecture, lab, deadline, sleep. Lecture, lab, deadline, sleep. Most days nobody asks why. Then one day you do, and the routine suddenly looks strange.

    One day the ‘why’ arises and everything begins in that weariness tinged with amazement.
  3. III

    Three ways to answer

    Escape means looking away and numbing the question. Hope means betting it all pays off later, which Camus calls the leap. Revolt means keeping the question open and living fully anyway. Camus picks revolt.

  4. IV

    Sisyphus, happy

    The gods gave Sisyphus the worst punishment they could think of, endless and useless work. Camus turns it around. Sisyphus knows exactly what his fate is, and knowing it makes it his.

    One must imagine Sisyphus happy.
