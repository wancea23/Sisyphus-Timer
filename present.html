A presentation on Albert Camus

Why are you pushing it?

The Myth of Sisyphus, and the rock every student pushes

Open with a question to the room: who has an exam or a deadline this week? Then say that Camus wrote a whole book about that feeling. Keep it under 30 seconds.

The myth

Who was Sisyphus?

He chained Death

The craftiest king of Ephyra put Death in chains, and for a while nobody on earth could die.

He tricked the underworld

Allowed back up for a moment, he saw the sun and the sea again and refused to return.

The punishment

The gods gave him a rock and a hill. Every time he reaches the top, the rock rolls back down. Forever.

Tell it like a story, not a list. The point to land: the gods chose the worst punishment they could imagine, work that never ends and never matters.

Camus’ starting point

The absurd is born of this confrontation between the human need and the unreasonable silence of the world.

We want life to make sense. The world doesn’t answer. That collision is the absurd.

Read the quote slowly, then say the plain version underneath. The absurd isn't in us or in the world, it's in the clash between the two.

Pause after the third line. Ask: has anyone here suddenly asked "why am I doing this?" in the middle of exam season? That moment is where Camus begins.

Three ways to answer

EscapeLook away

Scroll, sleep, quit. Act like the question never came up.

HopeBet on later

It will all make sense after the diploma. Camus calls this the leap.

RevoltPush anyway

No guarantee of meaning. You push with your eyes open, and the effort is yours.

Camus chooses revolt.

Make sure everyone remembers these three words: escape, hope, revolt. The class will vote on them in two slides.

The same rock, seven worldviews

  • CamusPush anyway. The struggle is enough.
  • NietzscheWould you push it again forever? Then love it.
  • KierkegaardThe meaning lies beyond the mountain. Take the leap.
  • Marcus AureliusThe obstacle is the way. Do your part well.
  • EpicurusWhy climb at all? Live quietly, avoid needless pain.
  • ConfuciusOne basket of earth at a time. Stopping is your choice.
  • ZenChop wood, carry water. Be fully here for this step.
This is the worldview comparison. Point out that only Camus refuses both the hope beyond and the escape from the rock.
One must imagine Sisyphus happy.
